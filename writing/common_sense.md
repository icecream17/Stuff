# common sense

when I imagine someone congratulating me on having common sense, I think, well, that's just my perspective and experience.

Connotatively, I feel like 'common sense' is simply a perspective of an ideal who is grounded with the world yet not necessarily an expert in any particular thing.

Therefore common sense can be wrong and outdone by experts in a field. I guess the notion put forward by the idea of 'common sense' is that experts often make blunders such that common sense wins anyway.

My programmer math brain simply jumps to the obvious childish conclusion to combine common sense with expertise (or alternatively, they exist separately but work together) to get something actually ideal.

Anyways this was just a random thought of me realizing the notion of common sense doesn't exactly synchronize with me anymore.

## ai review

i asked chatgpt to review my thought above. Basically it only contributed that "common sense" is a vague term that could have different meanings, it listed a few:

> - what most people in my environment regard as obvious
> - basic reasoning that is useful regardless of expert knowledge
> - practical wisdom of how the world works

and in particular the first one has the logical possibility/gap "this seems obvious, but maybe I'm missing something"

so that basically explains kinda where the thought comes from, but chatgpt was too agreeing to be of more use. Asking grok if there was more I then see

- "common sense" is intuition is knowledge so deep it doesn't feel like it is still knowledge based on regularities. Expert knowledge is based on the regularities of their field. So all together, what regularities are correct?
   - this feels like a more precise question
- experts who test themselves against the real world is highly trustworthy
   - my instinct to combine was correct, but the combination is asymmetric: it's not a 50/50 split
   - someone who lives in their expertise long enough, treats their expertise as common sense inside their world. And they're kinda right, even though outside the world it can be very unintuitive.
- "common sense" is just the name for a default model of the world (i.e. chatgpt definition 1). Realizing that, it loses its moral force and just becomes a description.

While useful for anyone reading this, bringing in ai turned out to take too long for me personally. Since the thought was just random after all, so it's not particularly important.
